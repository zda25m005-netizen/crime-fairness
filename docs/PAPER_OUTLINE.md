## The one-sentence claim

Everything in the paper has to serve this. If a paragraph doesn't, cut it.

> Reported group disparities in crime prediction are largely a function of how
> often crime is recorded in each region and of how the metric was aggregated,
> not of how well the model serves each region — and we can predict the size of
> the artifact in advance, show it in five cities, show it inside the fairness
> libraries meant to fix it, and we found it first in our own retracted result.

## The four things that make it publishable

Keep these visible; a reviewer skimming should hit all four.

1. **Predictable, not just present.** `A_F1` forecasts the observed gap at
   r = +0.94 before any model exists. Most critique papers can only say "we
   found a problem"; you can say "and here is its size in advance."
2. **Replicated.** Five cities, one experiment, no model trained.
3. **Inside the remedies.** The libraries recommended as the fix carry the
   same confound, and all three method families fail differently.
4. **Self-applied.** You retracted your own headline result with this method.
   This is the credibility anchor. Do not bury it.

## Section by section

### 1. Introduction (1.5 pp)

Beats, in order:

1. Open on the concrete: a fairness audit reports a 41-point F1 gap between
   dense and sparse regions. Ask what that number means.
2. State that the model in that example has *identical* skill in every region
   by construction, so the honest gap is zero.
3. Name the two mechanisms (base-rate dependence; pooling over nested units).
4. State that you found the second in your own work and retracted it. One
   sentence. Do not apologise, do not dramatise.
5. Contributions list, four items, matching the four above.
6. Last paragraph: what this paper does **not** argue — that predictive
   policing is fair or harmless. Put this in the intro, not only in the ethics
   statement, because that is where a hostile reader forms their impression.

### 2. Background and related work (1.5 pp)

Concede everything that is not yours, early and clearly:

- **Davis & Goadrich (2006)** — base-rate dependence of precision/recall. The
  mathematics is theirs.
- **Kleinberg, Mullainathan & Raghavan (2016); Chouldechova (2017)** — the
  impossibility result. Yours is narrower: it is the library *default*, and
  the cost is measurable in flagged places.
- **Lum & Isaac (2016); Richardson, Schultz & Crawford (2019); Ensign et al.
  (2018)** — recorded crime is not crime.
- **Wang, Kapoor, Barocas & Narayanan**, *Against Predictive Optimization* —
  adjacent enough that omitting it looks careless.
- **The Markup (2023)** on Geolitica; **Saunders, Hunt & Hollywood (2016)** on
  Chicago's Strategic Subject List. Both support "there isn't much accuracy to
  trade."

The gap you fill: this literature is about the *data* and the *deployment*.
Almost none of it is about whether the reported evaluation numbers mean what
they appear to mean.

### 3. Threshold metrics track base rates (1.5 pp) — **Fig 1**

- Derive `A_F1(p) = 2p/(1+p)`. Emphasise it is a **floor, not a ceiling** —
  a model can and should beat it. State that it is achieved by answering yes
  to everything.
- Worked numbers: 75.0 at p = 0.60, 21.4 at p = 0.12 → 53 points free.
- Note AUC's base-rate invariance, which is why it is the control throughout.
- **Fig 1 left**: the curve with each city's Head and Tail marked.
- **Fig 1 right**: predicted vs observed, r = +0.94, with the macro AUC gap
  sitting on zero on the same axes.

### 4. Pooling inflates (1.5 pp) — **Fig 5**

- Define the nesting. Rows are (unit, time); units differ in base rate.
- State and prove the invariance: macro AUC is unchanged under any per-unit
  monotone transformation, so between-unit ranking cannot contribute. Short
  proof or a clean argument — this is the technical core.
- The sharpest single demonstration: a scorer knowing **only** cell identity
  gets **pooled 66.67 / macro 50.45**.
- **Fig 5**: the β sweep. Within-cell skill constant by construction; pooled
  climbs, macro flat.

### 5. Five-city replication (2.0 pp) — **Fig 4**, Table 1

- Protocol: no model trained. An equal-skill scorer, d′ = 1, same noise
  everywhere. Truth is 0.00 in every column.
- Table 1: city | window | base gap | F1 gap | pooled AUC gap | macro AUC gap.
- Headline: **F1 gap +41.42 ± 4.87; macro AUC gap +0.46 ± 1.04.**
- **Disclose the per-city year windows and why** (LA's file starts 2020).
  Justify: the audit compares Head vs Tail *within* a city, never across.
- **Disclose the dropped cities and why** — Austin (no lat/lon), Baltimore
  (endpoint dead), San Francisco (403 to automated clients; we did not
  circumvent). This costs nothing and buys a lot of trust.

### 6. The published literature (1.0 pp) — Table 2

**Your weakest section. Reviewers will attack n = 6.** Either strengthen it or
frame it hard as suggestive.

- State the inclusion criteria explicitly and up front.
- Report both parametric and rank-only correlations; report the negative
  control (ST-HSL, non-significant) prominently — an internal negative control
  is the best defence you have here.
- Frame as: consistent with the mechanism established in §3–5, not as
  independent proof.

### 7. Inside the fairness toolkits (2.0 pp) — **Fig 2**, Table 3

- Same equal-skill construction, so truth is again zero.
- **Metric functions first**: Fairlearn's `MetricFrame` reports ~52 points of
  precision disparity and ~23 of F1 on identical ROC curves; AUC reports <1.
- **Three families, three different failures** (Table 3):
  - post-processing — AUC unchanged, harm redistributed (Tail FPR 13.62 →
    38.53 under AIF360 `RejectOption`)
  - in-processing — accuracy 72.11 → 65.79 at a tight DP bound
  - pre-processing — AUC 76.35 → 69.68, and *creates* a +19.32
    equal-opportunity gap where there was −2.16
  - `Reweighing` does nothing at all; `PrejudiceRemover` at low η reports a
    *higher* AUC than unconstrained by using the group indicator it was handed
- **State the direction, not just the magnitude.** Under `demographic_parity`
  the TPR gap is **−21.26**: the sparse group gets the higher TPR *and* the
  higher false-alarm rate. We described this by magnitude alone for a week and
  it read backwards.
- Say plainly that `equalized_odds` and TPR-parity behave correctly. A blanket
  indictment is less credible and less useful than a specific one.
- **Fig 2**: the base-rate-spread sweep — the analyst's grid choice sets both
  the reported unfairness and the damage the remedy does.

### 8. Real data, end to end (1.5 pp) — **Fig 3**

The section that answers "this is all synthetic."

- Chicago burglary 2015–19, 61,640 records, 262 weeks, 249 cells.
- **Operating point is a patrol capacity (top 20%), not a 0.50 threshold.**
  Explain why: at 0.50 the model flags 100% of Head and every downstream
  number is meaningless. This is a modelling-honesty point worth making.
- Table: pooled vs macro per group. Inflation **largest in the Tail (+18.18)**.
- **Two claims to land hard:**
  1. Pooled AUC nominates the sparse region as *best*-served (71.72); macro
     says *worst* (53.54).
  2. Pooled and macro disagree on the **sign** of the gap (−9.82 vs +2.23) and
     therefore nominate different regions for remediation.
- Then `demographic_parity`: Tail goes **0 → 3,528** crime-free cell-weeks
  flagged, on a model with macro AUC 53.54 — it has no idea which of those
  places is worth visiting.
- **Fig 3**, both panels.

### 9. What to do instead (1.0 pp)

The constructive turn. Reviewers reward this and you currently under-serve it.

Give a **named, numbered checklist** so it is citable:

1. Report macro (within-unit) alongside any pooled metric, and state the unit.
2. Report `A_F1(p)` alongside any F1, per group.
3. State the operating point as a capacity, not a probability threshold.
4. Report the base rate of every group.
5. Before claiming a disparity, check it against the zero-skill prediction.

Then `gapcheck`: `pip install gapcheck`, one call, runs on any grouped
evaluation, exit code gates CI. Mention that the invariance is asserted in its
test suite. **Anonymise the repo link for submission.**

### 10. Limitations and our own retraction (0.75 pp)

Methodology limitations go **here in the body**, not in the endmatter — the
FAccT author guide is explicit about that.

- Recorded crime is not crime; base rates are themselves policing artifacts.
  Your argument is **conditional** and this objection strengthens it.
- Burglary is less discretionary than drug offences but still
  reporting-mediated.
- n = 5 cities; n = 6 published models.
- The equal-skill construction is synthetic *by design* — it is what gives you
  ground truth. §8 supplies the real-data arm.
- The retraction: +11.11 Tail advantage and +2.81 physics gain were this
  artifact; corrected to +0.16 ± 2.76, 4/8 bins. Report it plainly.

### 11. Conclusion (0.25 pp)

Short. End on the point that a metric that reports 41 where the truth is 0 can
equally report 0 where the truth is large — unreliability is not exoneration.

Not front to back. Write §5, §7, §8 first — they are tables and figures you

last, when you know what you actually proved. §9 and §10 are quick once the
